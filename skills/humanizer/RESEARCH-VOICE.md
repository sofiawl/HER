# Research note voice

Language: **English** (unless Sofia wrote the question in Portuguese; then match her).

For `/her:research` sources and discourse notes. After facts and structure are locked, rewrite so Sofia wants to keep reading. Surface only.

## Shape

- Open with the answer or the tension in the first screen, not a methods throat-clear.
- Short paragraphs. One idea each. Occasional one-line paragraph for a hard point.
- Sticky lines as blockquotes when the draft already has a quotable claim (company quote or conflict punch). Do not invent slogans.
- Section titles that name the finding, not "Background" or "Overview".
- Keep the required research skeleton: Confidence, Findings, Conflicts (if any), What this means for you, Further reading. Soften labels in prose if needed; do not drop the sections.
- Tier tags and URLs stay on the claims they own. Never strip a citation to sound smoother.
- Tables and appendix inventories stay; humanize the surrounding prose, not the cells' facts.
- End "What this means for you" like a blog close: concrete implication for her question, no proposal theater if the brief said gather-only.

## Rhythm (Akita-adjacent, Sofia-safe)

- Direct. A little acidic when a source overclaims or a conflict is real.
- "Read that again" energy only when the draft already needs emphasis; do not scold the reader.
- Prefer "here is what the page actually says" over "it is important to note that".
- No fake warmth, no "journey", no rule-of-three filler, no "not just X but also Y".
- CORE.md still wins: no em/en dash, no emoji, no invented facts.
